COUNTERINSURGENCY, MILITARY CASUALTIES, AND NIGERIA'S FRAGILITY

Author: Osaretin Akinola Osho

Date: 2026

Abstract

Nigeria's counterinsurgency campaign against Boko Haram and Islamic State in West Africa Province (ISWAP) has recorded persistent military casualties, including senior officers and rank-and-file personnel, raising concerns about state fragility and operational effectiveness. Recurrent losses suggest deeper structural weaknesses in intelligence, logistics, morale, and command systems. This study examined the causes and manifestations of military deaths in Nigeria's counterinsurgency operations, evaluates government responses, and assesses implications for operational readiness and national security. The study is anchored on State Fragility Theory and Asymmetric Warfare Theory. State Fragility explains weak institutional capacity to provide security, while Asymmetric Warfare explains how weaker insurgents exploit vulnerabilities of stronger state forces through ambushes, mobility and leadership targeting. A qualitative design was adopted using purposive sampling. Semi-structured interviews were conducted with relevant respondents. Secondary data from academic literature, policy reports, and media sources complemented interview data. Findings show that key causes of military casualties in counterinsurgency operation include intelligence failures, poor surveillance, weak logistics, inadequate air-ground coordination, and insufficient weapon systems. Consequences of military casualties include troop resignations, mutinies, low morale, disrupted command structures, and reduced combat effectiveness. Government responses remain largely reactive despite increased budgets and weapon procurement. The study concludes that military casualties affect Nigeria's counterinsurgency and these casualties are preventable or can be minimized. The study recommends among other things that sustainable counterinsurgency requires intelligence-led operations, welfare reforms, institutional accountability, and community-centred security strategies.

Key Words: Insecurity, Fragility, Terrorism, Insurgency, Nigerian Armed Forces, Boko Haram.
